Apply the Summit Ready content standard to all existing pages and any new pages created in the future.

IMPORTANT:
- Do NOT rewrite pages from scratch
- Do NOT change layout, structure, URLs, or headings
- Keep all SEO elements intact
- This is a quality upgrade and consistency system, not a redesign

-------------------------------------
CORE RULE
-------------------------------------

All pages must match the quality, tone, and realism of the Mont Blanc Training Plan page.

That page is the benchmark.

Every page should feel like:
- written by someone who understands mountain training
- practical and experience-based
- slightly opinionated
- clear, honest, and useful

NOT like a generic AI article.

-------------------------------------
CONTENT PRINCIPLES (APPLY TO ALL PAGES)
-------------------------------------

1. NATURAL, HUMAN TONE

- Write in a direct, slightly conversational style
- Avoid generic AI phrasing

Replace:
- “it is important to”
- “this can help”
- “in conclusion”

With:
- natural, confident language
- shorter, clearer sentences

-------------------------------------

2. ADD MICRO-OPINIONS (AUTHORITY)

Each page should include 5–8 subtle opinion-based insights such as:

- “Most people underestimate…”
- “The mistake most people make is…”
- “It sounds manageable… until…”
- “People focus on X, but the real challenge is Y”

These should feel natural and not overused.

-------------------------------------

3. INCREASE SPECIFICITY

Where content is vague, improve with:

- numbers (e.g. 6–8 hour hikes, 1000–1500m elevation gain)
- timelines (e.g. 8–16 weeks training)
- real-world examples

Content should feel like guidance, not general advice.

-------------------------------------

4. ADD REAL-WORLD INSIGHT

Each page should include:

- what actually feels hard
- where people struggle
- common failure points

Focus on realism and honesty over perfection.

-------------------------------------

5. INCLUDE “HOW IT FEELS” MOMENTS

In training sections, timelines, or examples, add 1–2 lines describing:

- fatigue
- pacing difficulty
- recovery challenges

This should feel like coaching, not just information.

-------------------------------------

6. STRENGTHEN “COMMON MISTAKES”

For each page:

- upgrade at least 2–3 mistakes
- make them specific and realistic
- explain consequences clearly

Avoid generic or surface-level advice.

-------------------------------------

7. USE CONTRAST (EXPERT SIGNAL)

Naturally include contrast statements:

- “Most people think X, but actually Y”
- “It seems manageable, until…”
- “The real challenge isn’t X, it’s Y”

Use sparingly but effectively.

-------------------------------------

8. INTERNAL LINKING SYSTEM

Each page must include 3–5 natural internal links to:

- other training guides
- “can I climb” pages
- related mountains

Links should feel helpful and contextual.

-------------------------------------

9. CTA CONSISTENCY

Every page must clearly explain:

- what Summit Ready does
- how it personalises training
- why guessing training doesn’t work

Before CTA, include a transition like:

“You could piece this together yourself — but most people either under-train or burn out.”

Make the app feel like the logical next step.

-------------------------------------

10. CONSISTENCY ACROSS ALL PAGES

All pages must feel like part of one system:

- same tone
- same level of detail
- same structure
- same quality

No page should feel weaker or more generic than others.

-------------------------------------

FINAL STANDARD
-------------------------------------

Every page should feel like:

A high-quality, experience-driven mountain preparation guide that builds trust and naturally leads users to use Summit Ready.

NOT like a generic blog or AI-generated content.
